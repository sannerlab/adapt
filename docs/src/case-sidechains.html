Missing side chains

Rebuild them from a rotamer library, check or choose the rotamer in 3D, or keep only the atoms the experiment saw.

When some side-chain atoms of a standard amino acid are missing, ADapt lists the residue under sidechains to complete, with the atoms it will build (build: CG CD NE CZ NH1 NH2). By default each such side chain is rebuilt when the receptor is written: every rotamer of the library is scored for favourable contacts and clashes with its surroundings, and the best is used, unless you choose another.

See and choose the rotamer

The drop-down lists every rotamer of the library. Choosing one redraws it and gives its score, and it is the rotamer built. You can also hover over the drop-down and turn the mouse wheel to step through the rotamers one by one, watching the side chain and the score change:

The rotamer the drop-down shows when the receptor is written is the one built: auto, the best-scoring, unless you chose another. A chosen rotamer is built in the residue's symmetry copies too, recorded in the session, and named in the receptor's header (REMARK 850 AGFR rotamer D:ARG:100:_ ARG 3 of 81). Going back to auto undoes the choice. On the command line: agfr --rotamers "D:ARG:100::3".

Or keep the side chain as it is

Right-clicking a residue under sidechains to complete offers Complete sidechain (the default) and Include as is. The same menu on the category applies to all of its residues.

Completing a side chain far from the pocket changes little. Near the pocket it decides what the ligand will meet, so check the rotamer, or leave the side chain incomplete if its position is too uncertain to trust.
